The Pipeline

It used to take months and a committee to find out if an idea was worth building. Now it takes a weekend. The bottleneck didn't disappear — it moved.

HR Srinivas  ·  November 2026

Before AI, here's what happened to ideas — process improvement, system development, process automation, you name it. Any idea.

You took the idea. You sold it up the chain. Someone above you sold it further. Eventually a yes came back — go figure out if it can be built.

You went to IT with the Yes. Who told you why it couldn't be done: another system already doing something similar. Data privacy. Competing priorities. No bandwidth until Q3. You get the drift.

If you got past that gate, you needed money. If you got approval for the money, you started building with the following gatekeepers: business analyst, solution architect, developers, tester, security review, UAT.

And somewhere in the middle of all this, scope crept. Because by the time anyone saw what it actually looked like, everyone had one more thing to add.

Then one of scores of things happened. Business priorities changed. Or a regulation shifted. Or something else that made the whole thing redundant, less attractive, not worth the time and effort — taking down months of effort with it.

Net result: nobody wanted to experiment with an idea that could end up in smoke.

If none of that happened and it actually shipped? You had to convince people to use it. And that's a whole other story.

Pre-AI, that was the pipeline. For one idea.

Cut to now.

You're sitting at your desk — maybe literally on the pot. Idea drops. You open a terminal. Spec it out in a conversation. Get a rough outline. Spin up an HTML prototype for the look and feel. Build a scope document. Go back to the terminal.

Build the thing. In a weekend.

Deploy it. Run it. And then — this is the part nobody talks about — look at it.

Really look at it.

Either you realise it doesn't look all that great when it comes off the assembly line. So you — the originator — kill it. Yourself. No committee. No post-mortem. No sunk cost spiral.

Or you realise you're onto something. And now the conversation starts — but this time you're not selling a concept. You're showing a working thing.

Nine die. Not on paper. Not in a committee. After you built them and saw them and decided they weren't worth your time.

One makes the cut. That one arrives at every meeting already real.

Now multiply this by every empowered employee in your organisation who has an idea and a weekend.

That's not a productivity gain.

That's an innovation pipeline that has never existed before at this cost, at this speed, and at this scale.

The only thing standing between that and your organisation is whether the gatekeepers allow it. The bottleneck didn't disappear. It moved. It's now you.
