The Furious Mind

AI didn't give the fast mind more to think about. It finally gave it somewhere to put it.

HR Srinivas  ·  November 2026

The "AI causes cognitive decline" argument assumes everyone thinks the same way.

It doesn't.

The person who outsources thinking to AI and accepts whatever comes back? That person was already copy-pasting their way through work. AI didn't create that habit. It just gave it a new interface.

But there's another person the decline crowd never models.

The one who thinks faster than they can act. Who has five ideas before finishing one task. Who was told — repeatedly — that the problem is focus. Do one thing. Finish it. Move on.

That person didn't have a thinking problem. They had an execution bottleneck.

Pre-AI, the only way to operate was to slow down. Swallow the frustration. Watch half the ideas die in the queue.

Now?

Five tabs. Five projects. Five AI conversations — each one being steered, challenged, redirected, corrected. Not delegated. Conducted.

That's not cognitive decline. That's the first time in that person's life that the speed of doing finally caught up to the speed of thinking.

AI didn't give the fast mind more to think about.

It finally gave it somewhere to put it.

The zombie outsources to AI and accepts what comes back.

The furious mind interrogates it, rejects drafts, pushes harder, context-switches across five problems — and builds more than was ever possible alone.

Same tool. Completely different cognitive outcome.

AI is not making everyone dumber. It's making the already-passive more passive. And the already-sharp, sharper.

The gap between the two just got wider. That's worth paying attention to.
